Visual development and spatial puzzle brainstorming for the FIRST scene of a serious story about three Viking-age Norse friends, Erik, Baleog and Olaf. Their regular alehall in a late9th-century coastal Norse settlement, historically grounded material culture. The player should feel a specific lived-in home before an uncanny descent. Strong human warmth, subdued worn material colors, rain, smoke, history and restraint. NO modern pub, NO neon, NO electricity, NO arcade machines, NO modern dartboards, NO Victorian city, NO highfantasy tavern cliches. Three wide horizontal panels exploring different useful side-view compositions of the same Viking-age alehall and small enclosed archery yard, with a final small cellar-collapse vignette.
Human-scale turf-and-timber longhouse, smoke-blackened rafters and central hearth, stone base, rough oak benches and trestle tables, shuttered openings, wool cloaks drying, clay and wooden ale cups, coopered casks, iron cooking hooks, shield rack, sleeping dog, a musician with a simple plucked lyre and locals who know the friends. Warm hearth/firelight, cool overcast coastal daylight through the smokehole/open door, subdued ochre/umber/peat/sea-grey. Normal adult-human Vikings, visibly distinct: sturdy armored Baleog with long beard, quick lean red-bearded Erik, genuinely chubby Olaf with round wooden shield and helmet. No dwarf proportions. Historically grounded clothing and equipment interpreted in rich dimensional16/32bit pixel-art language with tactile sculpted clusters, not flat cartoons or modern illustrations.
Playable physical beats to explore: Olaf pushes a heavy wooden ale handcart/cask sledge back into the cooper's storage bay using his shield forward, clearing the hall's working aisle. He raises his round shield to carry several wooden ale cups from the keeper to regulars at a table. Erik jumps using the parked cask/bench to reach a high loft beam and fetch an iron service key. Baleog uses his sword on a clearly taut soft lashing cord around a newly delivered salvage bundle with permission, and shoots his bow at a straw practice butt in the enclosed archery yard, a familiar casual challenge from a local. Keep civilians away from the firing lane. Make the tasks part of ordinary hall life, with familiar faces and quiet conversations, not three arbitrary puzzle switches.
Story turn: fishers have recovered a SMALL dark carved stone/bronze object tangled in their net. Its construction is strangely precise but has no identifiable modern design. It sits in a salt-stained salvage chest under a wool cloth on a low table near an old water-damaged cellar floor. The three friends gather because they recognize different impossible details: markings, material, a sound. Their combined weight causes decayed floor beams to crack and slowly tilt, then break, dropping allthree into the dark old drainage tunnel below. The cause is physical timber decay, not an explosion or magical portal. This find is the first intrusion of the unknown; the village itself is of their own time. Show the collapse as a connected side-view cutaway with real broken beams, falling earth and bodies.
Atmosphere: serious intimate narrative, friendship conveyed through practical help and familiarity, no parody slogans, no generated readable text/UI. Generate a fresh composition from this brief alone. This concept is for design exploration, not a runtime sprite sheet. Prioritize meaningful side-view geography, safe recoverable tutorial jumps, good human scale, welcoming inhabited spaces and a clear abrupt loss of safety at the end.
