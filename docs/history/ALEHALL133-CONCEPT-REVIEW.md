# Alehall133 independent concept review

Reviewed: `ALEHALL133.md`, `assets/pub133/ART-DIRECTION.md`, corrected prompt and `norse-alehall-concept.png`. Applied cooperative-world-design and native-sprite-art requirements. No runtime changes reviewed or made.

**Verdict: direction accepted for further design; resolve the following before implementation.** Familiar friends, practical help, a coastal household/community hall and an ordinary physical collapse provide a substantially better serious opening than the rejected modern concept. Warmth and modest teasing can establish relationships without making the scene a comedy.

## Period and tone

- Treat the keeper as a known host with household/community responsibilities, rather than transplanting a modern commercial pub and its customer-service routine. Establish whose hall this is and why these friends help there. Timber, wool, hearth, coopered vessels and working yard support the direction.
- The generated image still contains conspicuous diamond-paned windows, abundant decorative wall torches and a theatrical masonry undercroft. Do not carry these into production by default. Prefer shuttered openings, the door and roof smoke opening; use the central hearth as the principal interior source. Keep the specified simple plucked lyre legible rather than copying the concept's ambiguous instrument.
- **The cavernous vaulted sewer is the largest remaining tonal/historical problem.** A vast maintained brick-and-iron municipal network beneath this small Norse settlement should not be presented as ordinary ninth-century infrastructure. Begin below the boards with damp earth, timber supports and a modest stone-lined runoff/store cavity. If the existing sewer's enormous arches, valves and machinery remain, reveal them as an unexplained construction the friends do not recognize. This is a narrative recommendation, not an archaeological claim that every settlement used the same drainage arrangement. The concept's bottom panel is unsuitable as a literal period construction reference.
- Keep the uncanny object quiet: one precise impossible detail noticed differently by each friend is stronger than three successive ominous speeches. Establish a personal cost to leaving home and show the host reaching toward them during the collapse.

## Causality and tutorial

- Explain the key: a key owned by this hall should open its own storage chest, into which the fisher placed the recovered object. An unexplained local key fitting a sealed chest recovered at sea is an accidental mystery. Put the known key on an accessible storage peg/loft with an ordinary reason for fetching it; do not imply everyone normally jumps for daily tools.
- Resolve the cart/sledge/cask/bench terminology into one object and one visible climbing route. Parking clears an aisle and creates a step; that is a sound local consequence. Give it end stops and recovery space on both sides so it cannot trap a parked Viking or strand Erik.
- Serving cups and target practice should remain optional familiar activities, not invisible prerequisites for the keeper showing the find. Key and exposed binding may constrain opening the chest because they physically secure it. Do not turn the errands into a hidden three-task checklist.
- Show permission to cut the lashing and its exact attachment. A short binding held against wood naturally explains close sword work; avoid an arbitrary global rule that arrows cannot sever soft rope. The yard needs a backstop and controlled entrance, not merely civilians standing outside the initial line of aim.
- Four cups must fit the shield with visible stable contacts; spilling should reset only the serving attempt. Keep refill/retry immediate and understated. Provide a floor route back from every loft or cart position and safe waiting places away from the hearth, yard and collapse area.

## Collapse and remaining gates

Foreshadow localized rot without making the host knowingly summon friends onto an obviously lethal floor. Use a broad gathering area, visible increasing deflection and a committed short sequence once all three are present; exclude NPCs from the failing span. Preserve inspection/retry before commitment. Carry all three through one continuous collapse/fall into sewer stable ID10, avoid a second disconnected fall on arrival, and provide a believable survivable landing.

**Source gate: pending. Runtime gate: pending. Aesthetic gate: pending.** The concept image is a composition reference, not approved native artwork. Its painted detail and character proportions must not be downsampled into sprites. Runtime production still requires declared native sizes, separate source comparisons, adult scale, shield/cup contacts, moving supports, normal-control completion and spill/reload recovery at 1x/2x/coarse touch.

Historical calibration: the [National Museum of Denmark's Viking homes account](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/the-people/viking-homes/) describes timber longhouses with internal supporting posts, central hearths, roof smoke openings, hospitality and persistent damp/rot problems. It supports the restrained domestic direction; it does not establish the concept's monumental sewer as period infrastructure.
