# Scrap Orbit — reviewed concept

Status: visual brainstorm and design only. No runtime level or approved native
production sprites exist yet. Preserve this concept without downsampling it
into game assets. Independent critique: /root/orbit110_review.

The storm furnace's discarded iron enters a suspended scrap-sorting engine.
Magnetic coils hold its collection frames inside a clearly bounded field;
huge slow gears behind the service galleries turn the sorting mechanism.
It is a place with a purpose, not an assortment of disconnected floating ledges.

## Central rules

- Magnetic shoes are a physical, transferable inventory item. Any Viking can
  wear them. Erik's jump and running bash make him a useful explorer without
  making equipment arbitrarily character-exclusive.
- Soles adhere to visibly marked continuous steel service rails, including
  platform undersides and rounded floor-to-wall-to-ceiling corners. Body rotation
  follows the foot contact continuously. No instant switch or ceiling teleport.
- Olaf pushes iron frames along ordinary rollers into the suspension field.
  Their coils visibly energize; the same frame gradually lifts and seats into
  a matching orbital cradle. No object vanishes and reappears elsewhere.
- Camera and world gravity stay upright. Player shoes/working machinery turn;
  slow majestic background gears provide scale without fast counterrotation,
  strobing or a rotating camera. Local left/right follow the contacted rail.
- Baleog's arrow leaves in his actual bow-facing direction when turned, rather
  than always flying screen-horizontal. Rotating slotted guards expose physical
  rope restraints; hitting rope cuts rope. The latch and linked mechanism react
  visibly rather than switching an invisible progression flag.
- One main orbital engine evolves through the level. Do not add unrelated
  floating systems with different gravity rules merely for visual complexity.

## Progression

1. **Commission in safety.** Olaf pushes the first service frame into the field.
   The coil and cradle demonstrate capture/lift. Erik retrieves the shoes from
   a reachable service locker and tests a short curved magnetic rail with a
   recovery floor immediately below. Learn floor, wall and ceiling contact
   before any tight timing or hostile machinery.
2. **Ride, aim, dock.** Olaf's raised shield provides Baleog's boarding step.
   A gimballed service seat offers a quiet aiming position while the large
   orbital arm rotates around it; shoes also permit a deliberately rotating
   aiming face later. A slotted drum exposes an ordinary rope clevis. The first
   opening lasts1.2–1.5seconds with visible approach and a generous target.
   Releasing the restraint swings a docking pawl into the carrier's path;
   it slows progressively and catches at the upper rail. Erik transfers onto
   the underside route and clears a familiar visibly cracked ceramic cover,
   physically releasing a return ladder and creating a safe waiting shelf.
3. **Synchronize the departure.** Olaf pushes a second counterweight frame
   into the field's capture throat. Its actual descent pulls a departure cradle
   through a visible chain/pulley. Baleog times a second release through staggered
   rotating slots; Erik moves along the turning steel arm and ceiling route.
   Difficulty comes from timing and coordination, not a subpixel bullseye.
   Start with generous exposure and tune the final window through actual
   normal-input routes, including arrow travel time. Misses cost a short repeat
   cycle, not loss of the previous act. The completed machine parks, unfolds a
   return route and opens one ordinary service exit for all three.

## Production/review requirements

Retained concept: assets/orbit110/concept-raw.png, exact prompt beside it.
Concept illustration is not an asset density or collision approval. New sprites
must be generated at intended native dimensions and detail density using
skills/native-sprite-art. Define each platform's actual sole/rim, field boundary,
local rail tangent, turning pivot, cable endpoints and docking collision before
art production. Adult Viking height remains72world/36native pixels.

Test rotating foot contacts, corner traversal, release/falling without snapping,
inactive passenger transport, local bow trajectories, shutter exposure including
projectile travel, pause/resume, item transfer, missed-shot recovery and persistent
return ladders. Review native source, processed native and production1x/2x/mobile
evidence separately. A full healthy ordinary-control route and genuine failure/
retry are required before publication. Empty concept illustration or debug
progression cannot substitute for clearability.

The illustrated layout is a direction, not a frozen map. Main production risk
is simultaneous rotating mechanisms obscuring contact or target geometry.
Keep initial magnetic traversal and initial bow timing separate; combine them
only after players understand both. Parked heroes need stable safe surfaces.
