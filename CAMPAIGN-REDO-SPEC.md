# Last Call: The Return Address — campaign rebuild

## Narrative contract

This replaces Story only. Arcade remains available. Follow Astra's first proposal: the closing pub is reclaiming cabinet memory; inhabited worlds are being erased. The crew descends from the last credit through routing, memory, world construction, preservation, projection and ownership. They save an unfinished archive, sacrifice their reset copies, enter the three brass marquee bodies foreshadowed in chapter one, and escape into physical rain. Higher reality is material embodiment, not another screen. New scenes must reveal how the previous world was maintained.

Reuse crew controls, movement, inventory/HUD and sound infrastructure. Do not reuse existing background, terrain, prop, machine, enemy or door art for Story. Blank signs in generated art; runtime text remains readable. No freight yard, ghost house, dragon forge, cooking room, observatory or organ reskins.

## Persistent physical vocabulary

- Every Viking can USE a reachable handle, collect, carry, exchange and insert an item. No hero-ID check on handles or locks.
- A wide recessed pressure tread responds to any living body. A clearly connected gate retracts while held. A accessible ratchet handle beyond it permanently secures it, freeing the holder. Cable channels show the connection and animate energy flowing.
- A taut **frayed thread** is cut by sword. It connects directly to the restrained part. Cut ends recoil; the released part travels continuously along its visible track. Arrows cannot cut thick thread; this thickness must be legible.
- A circular target with a thin exposed glass diaphragm responds to arrows or a nearby sword. Neither requires a named hero flag. Targets are placed beyond reach when bow is needed.
- Heavy obstacles are never arbitrary dash/sword/bomb boxes. Dash only presses visibly sprung impact paddles; blasts only affect visibly cracked masonry. Omit these if a chapter cannot clearly communicate the material distinction.
- Shield blocking/stepping/floating retain global behavior. No special shield activation hotspot. Overhead shield is a support and slows Olaf's descent everywhere.
- Lifts have visible tracks, connected controls, acceleration, deceleration and collision surfaces at the rendered platform. Rider positions follow the same displacement. Repeatable return trip; no soft lock.
- Quest sockets match item silhouette and color. Wrong use preserves the item. Sources do not vanish until pickup succeeds; dropping remains recoverable. Required archive transfer is spatial and physical.
- Physical doors divide space; no teleport doors within levels. Final chapter transitions may use the existing exit sequence.

## Eight chapters and causal puzzles

1. **AFTER THE LAST CREDIT** — giant amber coin return below the closed pub. Brass warrior silhouettes above a distant grille foreshadow bodies. A tread opens the coin sluice; a teammate crosses and secures its ratchet. Olaf boosts Erik to a higher return shelf carrying a service wafer. Matching wafer socket opens the second shutter. A hanging thread restrains the final counterweight: cut releases it down its adjacent shaft, raising the shutter. Teaches tread/ratchet, shield support, inventory and visible causality.
2. **THE RETURN ADDRESS** — red and cream packet ribbons inside a routing loom. The game finish repeatedly circulates a parcel. Cross the routing gate using tread and ratchet. A distant exposed diaphragm releases the return track. USE the lift at its lower landing; all crew ride a moving packet cradle to the upper output. The return lever sends it down for stragglers. No freight cart or remote teleport. Teaches ranged interaction and revisitable transport.
3. **WHEN THE NAMES GO OUT** — ivory memory reeds in dark indigo pools, names vanishing from empty windows. A shield-assisted high handle secures the first memory gate. The upper reed carrier moves only after its local tether is cut. An eraser patrol and directed deletion pulses make front shield protection matter while the crew uses a nearby isolation handle. Safety alcoves let all heroes survive; combat can remove patrols. Teaches protection during cooperative work, not reflection onto a magic target.
4. **A WORLD BEING MADE** — mint ceramic extrusion beds building empty neighborhoods. A local thread holds a ceramic stamp. Cutting it lowers the stamp continuously into its nearby bed; completed travel—not the slash boolean—releases the gate. A repeatable lift takes all three above the machine. A separate tread/ratchet gate requires reunion on that upper deck. Teaches waiting for physical process and safe passage around a moving machine.
5. **ROOM FOR THE UNFINISHED** — woven copper archive cloth, small illuminated inhabited cells. Olaf's raised shield gives access to the archive shelf. Pick up the portable archive. Use the matched terminal to mount it: a visible carrier moves it from socket into a dock over time. The terminal returns the portable archive after securing the copy; completion requires collecting it, so the narrative cannot silently discard residents. A tread/ratchet route lets the other crew reunite. Teaches preservation and inventory continuity.
6. **THE SKY HAS A BACK** — underside of a projection canopy, midnight glass, cyan frame ribbons and shutter gills. No domes. Two vertically separated landings and a reversible lift give all characters a route. Bow reaches the exposed canopy diaphragm. Cutting the nearby shutter thread sends the shutter along its own track. Olaf floats from an upper ledge through a wide drop to a lower safety tread; Erik and Baleog use the reusable lift. The teammate secures the return gate before Olaf leaves the tread. Teaches asymmetric routes with reunion and reveals the pub beyond projection.
7. **WHAT A RESET COSTS** — matte charcoal ownership core, three copper output channels and visibly occupied reset memory. Mount the archive in a matching cradle. A reachable confirmation handle starts an animated drain of the three reset reservoirs. The tradeoff is stated in the story, not hidden in an action lock. Cooperatively secure the core gate. Three physical output cradles accept any Viking; occupation locks that character in place only after the other route is permanently open. All three occupied triggers transfer. No named hero terminals.
8. **WEIGHT OF RAIN** — brass bodies inside tangible pub marquee infrastructure. Giant screws, paper fiber, condensation; warm amber to blue daylight. A repeatable maintenance lift reaches the upper escape latch. A frayed cord releases a local drip gutter through continuous motion. Olaf shields falling leaks while the crew crosses a final tread/ratchet shutter. All three walk to daylight carrying the archive. End in real rain; no next cabinet portal.

## Continuous animation contract

Simulation is continuous; rendering snaps positions to the established two-unit grid. Use eased mechanism progress without nonlocal coordinates. The collision gate retracts with its rendered leaf. Released payload has one persistent position, from initial support through travel to destination. Only the final physical arrival enables the connected output. Lift carries riders by displacement before collision integration. Paused/inactive heroes remain simulated. Independent phases for unrelated animation. Stationary parts remain attached to their floor/track. Far layers fit the viewport at both zooms; native grid is selected for the actual display size, not forced to a tiny 256-cell atlas.

Create a new transparent sheet of platform materials, gate leaves, pressure treads, ratchets, thread spools, lift cradles, matching archive/wafer sockets, deletion drones and paper-fiber pub structure. No baked lettering, smoke or fire. Animate machine components in runtime from those new parts. Keep visual cues high contrast and consistent across palettes. Inspect actual screenshots at both zooms before claiming success. Preserve the corrected positive-weight normalization pipeline; never solve art defects by enlarging backgrounds.
