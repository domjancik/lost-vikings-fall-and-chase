# Worlds whose machines explain the puzzles

Start with why this place exists, what its mechanism processes, why it obstructs
the crew and what changes when they leave. The preferred arc moves from an arcade
pub into the machines, deeper systems/cyberspace, then a meaningful higher reality;
each new environment should express a new idea, not rename an old level. Serious
worldbuilding can live in architecture, machinery, wear and consequences instead
of long arbitrary lore blurbs. Human service doors, stairs and workspaces keep
the adult Vikings to scale beside enormous machines.

Use visual brainstorming to discover spatial mechanisms when helpful. Compare
concepts by causal clarity, new player verbs, contribution of all three heroes,
challenge pacing, recoverability and feasible high-quality animation. Keep
original concepts/prompts; generate runtime assets separately at native density
using [native-sprite-art](../native-sprite-art/SKILL.md). Brainstorm pictures are
not pixel-density-approved sprite sheets. Apply
[grounded-parallax](../grounded-parallax/SKILL.md) to depth layers.

Choose a distinctive core mechanism and evolve its use:

1. A safe commissioning area teaches one physical relationship through action.
2. A constrained application needs two abilities and shows their consequences.
3. A short combined challenge needs planning, positioning and timed execution.
4. An earned release/reunion lets all three reach the same exit.

Erik jumps, runs and bashes; Baleog shoots at range and cuts with a sword; Olaf
blocks/deflects, supplies a raised-shield walking plane and floats in drafts or
fluids. Cooperation comes from position, timing, carrying/support, redirected
forces and shared machinery. Do not merely assign three colored switches or
change what opens the same-looking box to force a different Viking. Material and
construction must clearly explain which action works: a soft exposed rope, an
armored arrow shutter, a cracked masonry plug, a pressure-driven diaphragm.
Any deviation from an established visual rule needs an unmistakable construction.

For each action, document input -> local mechanical change -> continuously
animated transfer -> observable result. Trace actual ducts, shafts, cables and
gear contacts. Inventive puzzles may need thought; they must not need guessing
invisible flags or arbitrary author intention. Keep hints hidden by default;
state animation and environmental structure should explain progress.

Challenge comes from increasingly demanding combinations, not long waits or
unannounced rule changes. Give waiting/parked heroes safe positions. Build recovery
floors, reversible controls and persistent commissioning progress where sensible;
failure should allow a readable retry rather than a softlock. Tune traversal and
hazards against real movement acceleration/jump envelopes. Show each hazard's
warning, active interval and recovery; examine simultaneous risks and switch time.

Before publication, independently critique puzzle causality, active/completed
state visibility, grounded machinery/enemies/posts, adult scale, connection ports
and every movement envelope using [VISUAL-REVIEW-GATE](../../docs/standards/VISUAL-REVIEW-GATE.md).
Use normal controls for a complete route with all three alive, plus relevant
failure/retry and state captures at 1x/2x/mobile. A successful flag, debug teleport
or static spawn screenshot cannot establish clearability or visual quality.
